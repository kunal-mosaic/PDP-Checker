## Persona
# Target Customer Persona

**Who they are:** Young Indian men experiencing early-stage hair loss, likely urban, possibly college students or early-career professionals. They're dealing with lifestyle factors common to this demographic—irregular eating habits, mess hall or quick meals, and recent life transitions like moving to a new city for education or work.

**Age range:** 18–26 years old.

**Key pain points:** They're noticing visible hair fall in daily life—strands on their comb, pillow, hands while shampooing, and shower drain. They've already tried multiple solutions that failed: home remedies (egg, onion paste, curd, curry leaves), various oils (coconut, almond, amla), and anti-hairfall shampoos. Nothing has worked. They're confused about the actual cause and frustrated by wasted effort. Many are in denial, waiting months hoping it resolves on its own.

**Motivations:** They want a simple, daily solution that actually works and is backed by science rather than tradition. They're looking for clarity on *why* their hair is falling and a credible fix that fits easily into their routine. They want to catch the problem early before it worsens, and they respond to proof—clinical claims, before/after results, and ingredient transparency.

**How they speak about their problem:** Casual, Hinglish-inflected language reflecting everyday frustration. Phrases like: *"Paani kharab hai," "mausam hai," "sheher change hua"*—external excuses they've been telling themselves. They describe the problem visually and emotionally: seeing hair everywhere, trying everything, still losing hair. There's underlying anxiety masked by deflection.

The narrative follows a classic problem-agitation-solution structure targeting young men (18-26) experiencing early hairfall. It begins with visceral recognition of the problem—hair on combs, pillows, shower drains—moves through the futility of traditional remedies, and culminates in a science-backed revelation: the true culprit is nutritional deficiency, not external factors.

1. **Visible Problem Recognition**: Hairfall shown through relatable, everyday moments

The narrative deliberately counters the "waiting trap"—blaming water, weather, or city changes—framing delay as risk.
